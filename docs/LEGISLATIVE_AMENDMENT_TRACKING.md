# NyayaSaathi (न्यायसाथी) — Legislative Amendment & Commencement Tracking

**Protocol Version:** 2024.2  
**Audit Date:** 2026-11-15  
**Scope:** Enactment Dates, Gazette Commencement Notifications, Repeal & Supersession Tracking

---

## 1. Distinction Between Enactment and Commencement

In Indian statutory interpretation, an Act passed by both Houses of Parliament and assented to by the President of India is not necessarily immediately operational. Many statutes include a **commencement clause**:
> *"It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint."*

### Case Studies Tracked in NyayaSaathi:

#### 1. Consumer Protection Act, 2019
- **Presidential Assent:** 9th August 2019
- **Commencement Notification:** S.O. 2351(E) published on 15th July 2020
- **Actual Effective Date:** 20th July 2020
- **Legal Effect:** Repealed and superseded the *Consumer Protection Act, 1986*.

#### 2. Code on Wages, 2019
- **Presidential Assent:** 8th August 2019
- **Enactment Status:** Passed and enacted.
- **Commencement Status:** Partial advisory commencement only (Sections 42, 67, 69 notified via S.O. 3772(E) on 18th December 2019 for the Central Advisory Board).
- **Substantive Wage Rules:** Staged pending coordinated state-level notifications.
- **Legal Rule:** NyayaSaathi grounds unpaid wage claims primarily in the *Payment of Wages Act, 1936* and *Minimum Wages Act, 1948*, providing explicit notes that the *Code on Wages, 2019* has been enacted and will repeal predecessor acts upon full commencement.

#### 3. Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019
- **Presidential Assent:** 9th August 2019
- **Commencement Notification:** S.O. 3110(E) published on 30th August 2019
- **Actual Effective Date:** 1st September 2019
- **Legal Effect:** Increased statutory penalties for traffic offenses, introduced Good Samaritan statutory protection (Section 134A).

## 2. Supersession & Repeal Tracking

When new legislation repeals an earlier statute, NyayaSaathi implements **non-destructive historical preservation**:
1. Predecessor statutes (e.g. Consumer Protection Act 1986) are retained in the database with status marked as `SUPERSEDED` or `HISTORICAL`.
2. Ongoing legal disputes instituted prior to the repeal date continue to reference the predecessor law pursuant to Section 6 of the *General Clauses Act, 1897*.
3. New citizen disputes are resolved under the current active enacted statute.
